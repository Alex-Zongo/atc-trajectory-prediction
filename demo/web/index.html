    How to read the probability regions

    At each of +20, +40 and +60 s the model's samples are turned into a density of the aircraft's position. The darker area, with a solid outline, is the smallest area holding half of that probability; the lighter area, dashed, holds 90%. Read them as: if the model is right, the aircraft will be inside the dark area at that time one time in two, and inside the light area nine times in ten.

    A region that splits into separate islands means the transmission supports competing instructions, for example a left turn against "another aircraft, keep flying". Each island's size follows its branch share in the numbered legend. A small region means the model is confident, not that it is right: the open circles show where the aircraft actually was, and the line above says whether it fell inside.

    The altitude chart uses the same idea over time: the band holds the central 50% and 90% of predicted altitude changes. With the default 12 Euler steps the 90% regions held the flown position about 86% of the time on the synthetic test set, so they are slightly too tight; 32 Heun steps bring this close to 90%.

    What this demo runs

    A semantic belief model (SBM) reads the transcript, each word's recognizer confidence, the aircraft's own ADS-B callsign and its last 30 s, and returns a distribution over structured instruction representations: whether the transmission addresses this aircraft, and its lateral, vertical and speed instruction. A conditional trajectory predictor (CTP) samples future trajectories under each sampled representation. The composed prediction draws K representations and S trajectories under each.

    Every model is the first training run of the paper's configuration. The selectors switch to the ablations and to the alternative trajectory representations of the paper's appendices.

    Data

    Flights are recorded ADS-B tracks from held-out test and validation segments over the San Francisco Bay Area. Transmissions are synthetic, generated from each segment's maneuver with simulated recognition errors. Positions are shown only relative to the aircraft or to the scene centre. No recorded speech is used.

    Limits

    • Heading, altitude and speed instructions only. Route, procedure, conditional, takeoff and landing clearances are outside the representation.
    • Each transmission is read alone, without earlier clearances or readbacks.
    • When the model reads no motion instruction, its prediction is often worse than the ADS-B-only predictor.
    • The predicted spread is narrower than the flown variation with the default sampler; 32 Heun steps bring coverage close to nominal on synthetic data. Not an operational tool.

    Typed transmissions are processed in memory and not stored.
